# Provisional shared speaker contract

The initial use case is friends sharing a local speaker. It is a proposed policy to evaluate, not an established product finding. Display names are separate from stable listener IDs. Likes and session acceptance are affirmative evidence; ordinary dislikes have negative utility but do not veto another listener's choice. Explicit blocks, eligibility, locked songs and mandatory caps are hard rules.

Representation defaults to at least one explicitly accepted song per listener. The user can explicitly choose best-effort representation; results then name every deficit. Missing profiles are rejected with the participant retained in the request. Unknown tracks are reported. Duplicate or aliased seeds add no weight. Unknown preference remains unknown; predicted acoustic affinity is displayed with its provenance and never becomes acceptance. Default discovery budget is zero; opt-in discovery permits a stated number of tracks nobody has affirmatively accepted.

The fair objective first minimizes maximum and total representation deficit when best effort is selected. It then maximizes the worst listener's summed utility after service debt, maximizes mean utility, and improves directed edge value within a stated preference tolerance. All phases report their proof status. The separately labelled `affinity` research policy maximizes mean summed utility plus edge value. These internal quantities are not enjoyment or a probability of acceptance.

Each edge is `transition_weight * directed_affinity - same_folder_penalty`. A current-track anchor uses the same units. Folders are user-created categories, not artist labels. Unknown artists have no inferred artist grouping. Exact content duplicates share a recording family; alternate recordings need explicit family metadata. No cap disappears automatically.

Full eligible catalog ranking is the reference. A selection-only joint model protects a complete feasible covering set before the shortlist cutoff. Shared accepted tracks can cover multiple people. Locked tracks and incumbent candidates are retained. A shortlist can expand with an explanation, up to the 30-candidate request resource boundary. If full-catalog hard rules conflict, return the relevant acceptance sets and policy; a timeout does not establish infeasibility.

All algorithms consume the identical immutable instance. DP merges `(visited set,last)` states and is exact for this static policy up to 20 candidates. Beam and greedy are finite-width heuristics with safe necessary feasibility pruning; failure does not certify infeasibility. MILP has binary placements, continuous nonnegative edges, linking equalities, and lexicographic phases. Binary-edge mode exists only for equivalence experiments. Decoded queues and MIP starts pass the same independent validator.

The pinned CBC 2.10.3 bundled with PuLP 3.3.0 showed a negative-maximization MIP-start discrepancy in the new exhaustive fixtures. The formulation therefore minimizes the negative canonical phase expression, with bound signs mapped back to maximization units. Tests cover negative scores and both edge formulations. CBC warm-start acceptance is recorded from logs; initialization alone is not treated as acceptance. PuLP solution status, log termination and gap tolerance all participate in proof reporting.

Requests are bounded to 8 listeners, 20 slots, 30 shortlisted tracks and 60 seconds. The deadline includes validation, full ranking, candidate feasibility, construction, initialization, solve and post-validation. CBC sees the remaining budget. Process startup and validation can overshoot by a small measured overhead; this is a cooperative computational budget, not a realtime deadline or a production latency promise.
